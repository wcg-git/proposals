What changed, and why

Every change responds to a pattern in the current registration data. Figures are in the accompanying proposal.

  1. County is asked first, not last. Roughly one in five registrants lives outside the funded counties. Asking first identifies them before they take a limited seat, and routes them to a waitlist rather than a rejection.
  2. One session per registration. Attendance per seat is roughly three times stronger for people who pick a single session than for those who pick several. Multi-select was manufacturing seats nobody intended to use.
  3. A second session is offered after confirmation. Multi-session registrants still supply most unique attendees, so the cross-sell is preserved deliberately, just after one real commitment exists.
  4. Seat counts are real. Sessions hold 30 live seats virtually and 50 in person. Registration is opened beyond that on purpose, because roughly one in five registrants attends. Spots shown are true remaining registration spots against current sign-ups, so nothing is invented and growth is never throttled.
  5. Registration closes two hours before start. A real deadline creates urgency without limiting how many people can register.
  6. An explicit commitment checkbox and one-click reschedule. Turns a passive sign-up into a stated intention, and gives a no-show an honest exit that frees the seat.
  7. A certificate that requires attendance. NBIC already issues certificates for other programs, so this is a reason to show up that costs nothing new to deliver.
  8. Built mobile first. Single-column layout, 48px touch targets, and correct keyboards for email, phone and ZIP.
Note on SMS: text reminders are not included. NBIC confirmed SMS will not be implemented, so the email sequence and calendar invite carry the reminder load.
