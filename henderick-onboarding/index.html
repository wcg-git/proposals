03 · Setting Up The Accounts

Step by step. In the order it has to happen.

Every account is created by Henderick and owned by Henderick. Walton Creative Group is added afterward as a partner at the company level rather than as individuals. Agencies often do the reverse and own the accounts themselves. We do not work that way.

You should be able to work through this without us. If anything stalls, email Aaron and he will work through it with you, or come by next week and finish whatever is left. Nothing here is urgent enough to be worth guessing at.

1. Google Business Profile

Start first · verification up to two weeks
Owner: Henderick

This one starts first purely because of the waiting. Nothing visible happens while Google verifies, so the sooner it begins the sooner it is behind us.

  1. Sign in to google.com/business using the Henderick marketing address, not a personal account.
  2. Search for Henderick. If a profile already exists, claim it. If Google says someone else manages it, choose Request access and Google will email the current manager, who has three days to respond. Tell us if that happens.
  3. If no profile exists, create one. Business name, category, service area, phone, and website.
  4. Choose a verification method when prompted. Google decides what is offered, usually a postcard, phone call, or video. Whichever appears, complete it.
  5. Turn on two step verification on the Google account before adding anyone. Google can revoke access without it.
  6. Open Settings, then People and access, then Add. Enter aaron@waltoncreativegroup.com and choose the Manager role.
Worth knowingManager is deliberately the right ceiling for us. It allows posting, responding to reviews, and updating hours, but not adding users or removing the listing. Those stay with you. Newly added people also wait seven days before certain actions, so adding us early avoids a delay later.

2. Google Ads

Twenty minutes · one step that matters
Owner: Henderick

The important part is simply who clicks create. An ads account created from inside an agency's account belongs to that agency. Created on your side, it is permanently yours and we are only linked to it.

  1. Go to ads.google.com and sign in with the Henderick marketing address.
  2. Choose Start now and create the account. If Google pushes you toward a guided campaign setup, look for Switch to Expert Mode, then Create an account without a campaign. You do not need to build a campaign today.
  3. Set the billing country to United States, currency to USD, and time zone to Central. None of these can be changed later.
  4. Add billing details. The card stays yours and we never have access to it.
  5. Find the ten digit customer ID in the top right corner and send it to us.
  6. We send a link request from our manager account. Approve it under Admin, then Access and security, then Managers. Requests expire, so approve it when it arrives.
Do not do thisDo not create the account by clicking a link or invitation from us. That is the one version of this that is painful to reverse. You create it, you own it, we request access afterward. We take Standard access. Admin and billing stay with you, and you can remove us at any time without involving us.

3. Meta, the Facebook side

Approval one day to one week
Owner: Henderick

Answer the first question before starting anything else here, because it changes the order of everything after it.

  1. Decide whose personal profile hosts this. Facebook requires a real person as administrator. Meta weights account age, history, and friend graph when deciding how much to trust an account, so an established profile is strongly preferred. A profile created this week that immediately builds a portfolio and begins advertising usually draws low spend limits and extra verification checkpoints. Waddell or Will, whichever of you already has one.
  2. Turn on two factor authentication on that profile first. Meta expects it before access can be shared.
  3. Go to business.facebook.com and create a business portfolio. Name it Henderick. Use the marketing address as the business email.
  4. Inside the portfolio, create the Facebook page for Henderick. Create it here rather than separately, so it is owned by the portfolio from the start.
  5. Open Business settings, then Business info, and start verification. Facebook will ask for the EIN and a card on file. Approval takes a day to a week.
  6. Create the ad account inside the portfolio. Business settings, then Accounts, then Ad accounts, then Add.
  7. Open Partners under Users, choose Give a partner access to your assets, and enter our business ID, which we will send you.
Worth knowingThe administrator role is invisible. Page and portfolio admins are never shown publicly, so this does not attach anyone's personal profile to the company pages. It also does not give Facebook anything about that profile it does not already have.

4. Instagram

Ten minutes · no waiting
Owner: Henderick

The easy one. Instagram has no account history requirement, so a fresh account carries no penalty. Before creating one, tell us what you remember about the existing account. Recovering it or starting clean are both fine. Knowing which is what matters.

  1. Create the account using the marketing address. We will send a few handle options to choose from rather than asking you to invent one.
  2. Convert it to a professional account. Settings, then Account type and tools, then Switch to professional account. Choose Business.
  3. In Meta Business Suite, add the Instagram account to the Henderick portfolio.
  4. Connect it to the Facebook page so both can be managed together.
Worth knowingAn Instagram account can only belong to one business portfolio at a time. If the old account turns out to be attached to someone's personal profile or another portfolio, it has to be released from there before it can be added here. That is usually the reason an old account feels stuck.

5. YouTube

Fifteen minutes
Owner: Henderick

YouTube has two separate layers of control and they are easy to confuse. Ownership sits on the Brand Account. Day to day access sits in Studio. Setting both up correctly now avoids an awkward conversation later.

  1. Signed in as the marketing address, create a channel and choose Use a custom name. That creates a Brand Account, which is what lets ownership exist separately from any one person's login.
  2. Open Settings, then Channel, then Advanced settings, then Manage permissions.
  3. Add a second owner from inside Henderick. This matters more than it sounds, because deleting the only owner's Google account deletes the channel with it.
  4. Add aaron@waltoncreativegroup.com as a Manager or Editor. Either lets us upload and publish without being an owner.
Worth knowingA channel that is not connected to a Brand Account can change managers but not owners, which is the trap most companies discover years later when the person who made the channel has moved on. Choosing a custom name at step one is what prevents it.

What this is built to protect

At the end of any engagement, whenever that comes, Henderick keeps both pages, the Instagram account, both ad accounts with their full spend history, the tracking data and every audience built from it, the Business Profile, and the YouTube channel and its content. Our access ends with one revocation per platform. Nothing has to be transferred, recreated, or negotiated. That is the entire reason for the order above.

Platform menus change often, and these names reflect what each platform called them in October 2026. If a label does not match what you are seeing, it has most likely moved rather than disappeared. Email Aaron rather than guessing, particularly on anything involving ownership.
