01 · The Opportunity

The hard part is already on the hard drive.

Sherry, Jeff, thank you for the conversation. And thank you to Dr. Parker Panovec, your Chief Medical Officer, for putting our name forward. It arrived alongside a kind word from a nonprofit partner we have worked with, which is the only sort of introduction that really counts.

Most projects at this stage are still trying to work out what the story is. Yours is already recorded.

You have fifteen people on camera. Patients, supporters, and the people who have carried this since the beginning. You have a B roll library that spans years of the clinic actually working. What you do not have yet is the ten minutes that puts all of it in front of a room and makes someone reach for a pen.

Twenty five years is a hard thing to hold in a program insert. Faith Family opened its doors on October 8, 2001, and more than thirty thousand patients have come through them since, across more than one hundred thousand visits. Read aloud, numbers like that pass by. On a screen, with a face and a voice attached, they stop a room.

That is the whole job here. Take what you already captured, find the arc inside it, and hand you a film that is finished, tested, and in your hands well before the morning of November 10.

05 · What We Deliver

The work, in concrete terms.

Four phases take this from a hard drive to a room.

01

Ingest, transcribe, and read everything

The drive comes to us. Every file is organized, transcoded to an edit friendly format, and backed up in two places before anything else happens. All fifteen interviews are transcribed in full. We read the transcripts and mark every line worth keeping, so the film is built from what your people actually said rather than from what anyone remembers hearing.

02

Story mapping with your team

We sit down together and build the film on paper. Which voice carries which chapter, where the founding era material goes, and what the room needs to feel in minute two and again in minute eight. You approve the arc before a single cut is made. This is the session that protects the November date.

03

Edit, finish, and shape

Assembly first, then a story cut, then the finish. Color correction across footage shot on different days in different light. Audio repair on interviews recorded in house. Titles and graphics in your brand. Music chosen for a room eating breakfast rather than for a trailer. Your team reviews online and we refine through the revision rounds in your tier.

04

Delivery and a playback check

Masters arrive in the formats your tier covers, named clearly, in a shared folder your team keeps. We also build the specific file the event room needs and confirm it plays clean ahead of time. A codec problem is easy to fix in October and impossible to fix in front of the room.
